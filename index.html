      What to use, and when

      Tap your situation to see what to look for on the label. We list ingredients, not brands, so this works with whatever is sold near you.

      Oily skin
      Look for
      A gel or foaming gentle cleanser, niacinamide (4 to 10%), an oil-free gel moisturizer, and a light gel or matte sunscreen.
      How to use
      Cleanse morning and night, then moisturizer, then sunscreen in the morning. Skipping moisturizer makes skin produce more oil, so keep it.
      Avoid
      Washing more than twice a day, harsh scrubs, and alcohol-heavy toners.
      Dry or flaky skin
      Look for
      A creamy, fragrance-free cleanser, hyaluronic acid, a ceramide moisturizer, and a cream sunscreen.
      How to use
      Apply hyaluronic acid to slightly damp skin, then seal it in with moisturizer right away. Use lukewarm water.
      Avoid
      Hot water, strong foaming cleansers, and exfoliating more than once or twice a week.
      Acne and breakouts
      Look for
      Salicylic acid (0.5 to 2%) in a cleanser or leave-on, benzoyl peroxide (2.5 to 5%) as a spot treatment, and an oil-free moisturizer labelled non-comedogenic.
      How to use
      Start with one active only, every second day, then build up. Benzoyl peroxide can bleach towels and pillowcases.
      Avoid
      Picking spots, using several strong actives at once, and heavy oils on breakout-prone areas.
      See a doctor
      If acne is painful, deep, leaving scars, or hasn't improved after 8 to 12 weeks.
      Dark spots and uneven tone
      Look for
      Vitamin C serum in the morning, niacinamide, azelaic acid, and a broad-spectrum SPF 50 sunscreen.
      How to use
      Sunscreen is the most important step, because sun exposure darkens spots again. Fading usually takes at least 8 to 12 weeks.
      Avoid
      Skipping sunscreen, scrubbing at marks, and "overnight whitening" products with unclear ingredients.
      Sensitive or red skin
      Look for
      Fragrance-free products with ceramides or centella (cica), and a mineral sunscreen with zinc oxide.
      How to use
      Keep the routine to three steps: gentle cleanser, moisturizer, sunscreen. Add new products one at a time, two weeks apart.
      Avoid
      Fragrance, essential oils, and layering many actives.
      See a doctor
      If redness, burning, or itching is constant or keeps coming back.
      Daily sun protection
      Look for
      A broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF 30 to 50. Mineral (zinc oxide) suits sensitive skin, and chemical formulas often feel lighter.
      How to use
      Use about two finger-lengths for the face and neck, every morning, even on cloudy days. Reapply every two hours when outdoors.
      Avoid
      Relying on makeup with SPF alone, or using a tiny amount.
      Dark circles and puffy eyes
      Look for
      Sunscreen around the eyes, a caffeine eye gel for puffiness, and a vitamin C or niacinamide eye product for pigment.
      How to use
      Dab gently with a ring finger and keep products off the lash line. Sleep and allergies matter as much as creams.
      Avoid
      Rubbing the area, and expecting creams to fix dark circles caused by genetics or the shape of the eye area.
      Dandruff and itchy scalp
      Look for
      Anti-dandruff shampoo with zinc pyrithione, ketoconazole, selenium sulfide, or salicylic acid.
      How to use
      Lather and leave it on the scalp for 3 to 5 minutes, two or three times a week, then rinse well.
      Avoid
      Scratching, and heavy oils on a flaky scalp.
      See a doctor
      If it is severe, patchy, or doesn't improve after about a month.
      Beard itch and razor bumps
      Look for
      A gentle shave gel, an alcohol-free aftershave with aloe, salicylic acid for bumps, and a light beard oil for dryness.
      How to use
      Shave after a warm wash, with the direction of hair growth, using a clean, sharp razor. Change blades often.
      Avoid
      Shaving very close, dry shaving, and picking at ingrown hairs.
      See a doctor
      If bumps become painful, filled with pus, or keep coming back.

      General guidance only, not medical advice. Patch test any new product on your inner arm first, and ask a pharmacist or dermatologist if you are unsure, are pregnant, or take medication.
